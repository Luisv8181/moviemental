# Ethics and Safety

Moviemental supports media discovery, education, reflection, and research. It is not a diagnostic tool, treatment service, crisis service, or substitute for professional care.

## Recommendation framing
- Say a work may resonate with an experience; do not promise that it will heal or help every viewer.
- Explain why a work matches a stated goal and describe limitations.
- Offer alternatives with different emotional intensity and narrative outcomes.
- Do not treat a character's choices as a universal model for recovery.

## Content warnings
Use specific, neutral, proportionate warnings for material such as suicide, self-harm, violence, abuse, sexual violence, substance use, eating disorders, psychosis, discrimination, bereavement, and medical trauma. Warnings describe content, not endorsement or a prediction of harm. Avoid unnecessary spoilers.

## Privacy
The initial catalogue should not require accounts or collect personal mental health histories. If future personalization is added, minimize data collection and avoid storing sensitive free-text disclosures by default.

## Representation
Avoid defining people by diagnosis or using mental illness as shorthand for evil or danger. Note supported examples of stigma, stereotypes, cultural context, and character agency. Lived experience is diverse, not uniform.

## Crisis boundaries
Never present a media recommendation as crisis support. If future features respond to crisis disclosures, prioritize appropriate support resources rather than recommending a film as the primary intervention.
