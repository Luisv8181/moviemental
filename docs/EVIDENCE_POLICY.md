# Evidence and Sourcing Policy

Moviemental separates observable content, creator statements, scholarship, and community interpretation.

## Source priorities
Choose sources appropriate to the claim: the work itself; official production material; attributable creator statements; peer-reviewed research; reputable journalism or specialist criticism; and community interpretation clearly labeled as such. Creator intent is not the same as clinical accuracy.

## Document important claims
Record the claim, evidence status, source IDs, a concise explanation of support, and meaningful limitations or disagreement. Summarize sources in original language. Do not copy long passages.

## Diagnosis labels
Use a diagnosis only when it is explicit in the work, supported by an attributable authoritative source, or presented as a sourced interpretation. Mark interpretations clearly. Never convert symptom-like behavior into a diagnosis without evidence.

## Experience labels
Experiential tags can describe grief, fear of rejection, shame, isolation, emotional numbness, avoidance, or difficulty trusting without medicalizing them.

## Uncertainty
Use unknown or leave a claim unasserted when evidence is insufficient. Do not fill gaps to make a record appear complete.
