# Recognition-first discovery

## Product principle

Moviemental should help people encounter a character or story that feels familiar to their lived experience. A person should not need a diagnosis, clinical vocabulary, or a desire to change in order to use it.

The primary entry point is a recognizable situation or feeling, for example:
- life changed and I do not feel like myself;
- people think I am okay because I hide my distress;
- I am trying to find where I belong;
- I want to be understood but struggle to open up;
- I am learning to live with loss or change;
- who I am does not match what others expect of me.

These are prompts for discovery, not labels applied to the visitor.

## Interaction rules

1. Let people start with plain language and everyday experiences.
2. Keep all selection/search interactions local to the page; do not transmit or store the visitor's personal disclosure.
3. Explain why a story was matched and allow the person to inspect the work and content notes.
4. Do not infer or assign a diagnosis to a visitor.
5. Make clear that similarity is subjective: a character may resonate without representing every aspect of someone's experience.
6. Keep content warnings visible and label unknown information rather than implying a story is safe.
7. Preserve alternate interpretations and cultural context in the registry.

## Current implementation

The homepage includes a recognition-first entry section with six experience prompts. Each prompt routes to the existing registry search. This is an early prototype: the search uses literal text matching, and prompt-to-query mappings require editorial evaluation. Some prompts may return few or no results until the underlying records are enriched with well-supported tags and aliases.

## Evaluation plan

Before describing this feature as effective peer support, test it with voluntary, low-risk usability sessions. Ask whether people understood why an entry matched, felt respected rather than categorized, could spot content warnings, and could easily broaden or clear their search. Do not ask participants to disclose diagnoses or traumatic experiences as a condition of testing.
