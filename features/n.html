Character feature · draft entry

N

A young idealist who believes Pokémon should be freed from people, and who has seen very little of the world he wants to change.

Pokémon Black and Pokémon White · video game · 2010 · Game Freak

Move left and right. The world of this story is split in black and white, and so is this page.

Experience · feeling different

He says he can hear them.

From his first scene, N speaks as if he understands what Pokémon are saying, and he talks about them as friends rather than tools. It sets him apart from the trainers around him, and it is the root of his conviction.

  • ListeningTreating every Pokémon as someone with something to say.
  • ConvictionIf they can be heard, he reasons, they should not be kept.

Move through the field to listen. Each small voice answers when you come close.

Experience · social isolation · late-story detail

The room he grew up in.

This section describes a late-game location. Turn on spoilers in the top bar to read it.

Late in the story, the player finds N’s childhood room: a bright space full of toys, a train set, a half-pipe, a basketball, but closed off from the outside world. It suggests a childhood spent largely apart from other people.

  • The toysA childhood that looks playful from the inside.
  • The wallsVery little contact with the world beyond them.
Mood · curiosity

Circles and formulas.

N is drawn to the Ferris wheel in Nimbasa City and to the mathematics behind things that turn and repeat. Order and pattern seem to make the world easier for him to hold.

Scroll to turn the wheel.

Theme · truth and ideals

Two convictions.

The game pairs two legendary dragons, one of truth and one of ideals. N and the player are each chosen by one. The story treats both convictions as sincere; the question is what each person does with theirs.

Experience · manipulation by a caregiver · late-story reveal

Who shaped him.

This section contains the story’s major reveal about N. Turn on spoilers in the top bar to read it.

Near the end, it becomes clear that the adult who raised N, Ghetsis, kept him isolated and fed him a single view of the world so he would serve as the public face of Ghetsis’s own plan. N’s ideals were sincere; the agenda behind them was not his.

  • The stringsBeliefs given to a child by someone who needed him to hold them.
  • Cutting themAt the end, N leaves to see the world for himself.

Moviemental records this as an experience of manipulation by a caregiver. It is the story’s portrayal, not a clinical description of abuse.
